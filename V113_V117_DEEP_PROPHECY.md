## Historical framing

The prophetic books are treated as historically situated literary collections rather than flat chronological biographies. Each guide distinguishes biblical narrative/theological claims from external historical reconstruction and avoids presenting disputed dates or identifications as settled fact.

### Song of Songs

Handled as lyric poetry rather than a continuous historical narrative. The guide emphasizes ancient Israelite imagery, agricultural and urban settings, love poetry, traditional Solomonic association and later allegorical interpretation.

### Isaiah

Structured around the Assyrian crisis, Jerusalem/Zion, Babylonian exile, Cyrus and post-exilic restoration. Particular attention is given to Hezekiah, Sennacherib, imperial politics, the Servant passages and new-creation imagery.

### Jeremiah

Centered on Judah's final decades, Josiah through Zedekiah, Babylonian domination, Jerusalem's fall, exile, prophetic conflict and restoration. Includes the temple sermon, Baruch, the new covenant and the historical appendix.

### Lamentations

Treats the five poems as trauma/lament literature arising from Jerusalem's destruction in 586 BCE, including acrostic structure, famine, social collapse, memory and hope.

### Ezekiel

Organized around the prophet's dated exile visions, Jerusalem's judgment, divine glory, oracles against nations, restoration, Gog and the final temple/land vision.
