## Historical framing

The batch treats Joshua through Samuel as a connected historical-literary sequence while avoiding the claim that the biblical narrative and archaeological reconstruction are identical. Joshua/Judges are particularly important for comparing conquest and settlement traditions with archaeology, geography and the emergence of early Israel. Judges is treated as a collection of judge cycles and later narratives with an editorial framework; Ruth is treated as a short family narrative set in the period of Judges; Samuel is treated as the transition from tribal leadership to monarchy and the rise of David.

The project deliberately distinguishes:

- what the biblical text narrates
- historical context that illuminates the text
- archaeological evidence
- external ancient evidence
- scholarly reconstruction
- theological/traditional interpretation

## Research direction

The historical-book model is intentionally compatible with passage-by-passage contextual study: ancient Near Eastern context, archaeology, geography and literary structure are treated as complementary evidence layers rather than as interchangeable proof. Joshua–Judges–Ruth and Samuel are also treated as a connected sequence, while recognizing that Jewish and Christian traditions organize and interpret these books differently.
